{\large\textbf{Research Brief (Gate G1) --- Does the virtual tumor board need a board?}}\\[2pt]
\small Vibhum Sharma (T32) \textbullet\ AI Tools for Research, Lab 7, Prompt 05 \textbullet\ 24 Oct 2026 \textbullet\ Labels: VERIFIED / INFERRED / UNKNOWN

\vspace{6pt}
\normalsize
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{3.1cm}L@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Review topic and key papers} & Lab~4 review: AI- and multi-agent-assisted virtual tumor boards (24 papers). Originals checked: Liu et al.\ 2026, EvoMDT, \emph{npj Digit.\ Med.} (PDF, VERIFIED); Kim et al.\ 2025, multi-agent MDT, MLHC (PDF, VERIFIED); Park \& Chae 2024, \emph{J.\ Gastrointest.\ Cancer} (PDF, VERIFIED); Kim et al.\ 2024, MDAgents, NeurIPS (abstract and code VERIFIED; tables INFERRED); Wang et al.\ 2024, ACL (abstract VERIFIED). \\
\textbf{Research gap} & Review Sec.~7 (4th gap): multi-agent systems report the best results but have the thinnest independent validation. EvoMDT compares its DeepSeek-based board only with single-pass runs of \emph{other} models (VERIFIED). Kim et al.\ 2025 tested roles and discussion on 31 private cases with an LLM judge and unequal call counts (VERIFIED). Untested: same backbone, \textbf{equal call budget}, public data, objective metric, small open model. \\
\textbf{Research question} & \textbf{RQ1:} with Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct fixed, is a 4-specialist tumor board (2 rounds + chair, 9 calls) more accurate than self-consistency with 9 samples on oncology exam questions? \textbf{RQ2:} does replacing specialists with generic agents change board accuracy? \textbf{RQ3:} does discussion change the vote, and how often does it turn correct members wrong vs.\ wrong members correct? \\
\textbf{Study pathway} & B --- controlled comparative study, with C-style ablations (roles removed; discussion removed) taken from the same runs. \\
\textbf{Dataset / protocol} & MedQA-USMLE 5-option test (MIT, VERIFIED), oncology subset n\,=\,97 by a frozen keyword filter (VERIFIED, hashed), pooled with MedMCQA dev oncology subset (MIT, VERIFIED; n UNKNOWN). Zero-shot: no training split, prompts debugged only on train items. Risk: benchmark contamination in pre-training (equal across arms). \\
\textbf{Baseline / comparators} & Same model for all arms: greedy CoT (1 call); self-consistency, 15 samples voted at $k$\,=\,1, 3, 5, 9, 15 (SC@9 = call-matched); generic-agent board (ablation). Own implementation (MIT); MedAgents, MDAgents and Kim et al.\ code are unlicensed, so used only as references. \\
\textbf{Primary metric and control} & Accuracy (higher is better; unparseable = wrong). Exact two-sided McNemar, $\alpha$\,=\,0.05, plus $\geq$\,2-point difference; paired bootstrap 95\% CI. Held constant: model, prompts' answer format, T\,=\,0.7, top-$p$\,=\,0.95, max tokens, items, seeds. \textbf{Power} (simulated, assumed 15--25\% discordance): n\,=\,97 detects $\approx$\,12--15 points; n\,$\approx$\,400 detects $\approx$\,6--8 points. Nulls are reported against this limit. \\
\textbf{Computing and risks} & Kaggle 2$\times$T4 (free), vLLM. Minimum run $\approx$\,1--3 GPU-h; full grid $\approx$\,5--20 GPU-h (INFERRED). Risks: vLLM on T4 untested (fallback: HF transformers); 7B too slow (fallback: Qwen2.5-3B); MedMCQA unavailable (fallback: MedQA oncology only, underpowered); quota (drop full MedQA, then seeds). \\
\textbf{First executable milestone} & \emph{Done:} oncology subset built from the real MedQA file (1,273\,$\rightarrow$\,97 items, sha256 recorded); filter audited before any model output; pipeline unit-tested with a mock model (29 tests pass). \emph{Next:} Kaggle smoke test on 5 MedQA train items, measuring per-item cost and parse failures. \\

\textbf{Contribution boundary.} Claimed: a call-matched, public-benchmark test of the specialist-board idea with an open 7B model, and spontaneous error-propagation rates. Not claimed: a new method, clinical validity, or anything about real tumor-board decisions. A null result is a valid outcome.
